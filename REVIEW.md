## 1. Fixed-parameter estimation vs. in-loop optimisation — **do not merge these**

These are two different experiments answering two different questions. They **disagree**, and the
disagreement is the pilot's main finding. Any summary that averages them is wrong.

| | **Stage A — estimation** | **Stage B — optimisation** |
|---|---|---|
| Question | Given fixed parameters, how accurately is the objective estimated? | Does an optimiser driven by that estimator find better parameters? |
| Parameters | Frozen, pre-optimised on the ideal simulator | Optimised in-loop by SPSA from a random start |
| Cells / runs | 2400 cells (2 tasks × 6 noise × 5 budgets × 4 methods × 10 seeds) | 40 runs (2 tasks × 1 noise × 4 methods × 5 seeds) |
| Scoring | \|estimate − exact reference\| | \|noiseless re-evaluation of final parameters − reference\| |
| Headline | Mitigation **helps**: REM 46/60 helps, 0 harms | Mitigation **does not help**; unmitigated is best on the converged task |
| Confidence | Reasonable (10 seeds, 6 noise settings) | **Weak** (5 seeds, 1 noise model, 1 of 2 tasks converged) |

**Stage A is the better-evidenced half of this pilot. Stage B is suggestive only.**

## 2. Preregistered vs. exploratory — corrected classification

`docs/research-questions.md` was written and committed before any data was collected. However,
**not everything now reported in Stage B was preregistered**, and the earlier HANDOFF text did not
make that distinction clearly enough.

### 2a. Genuinely preregistered (fixed before any data existed)
- RQ1, RQ2, RQ3 statements and hypotheses H2, H3.
- Stage A grid: tasks, 6 noise settings, 5 budgets, 4 methods, seeds `[0..9]`.
- Primary metric (absolute error vs exact reference) and the decision rule (non-overlapping 95% bootstrap CIs over seeds).
- The budget-matching rule and the requirement to report shots/executions/wall-clock.
- Kill criteria K1–K4.

### 2b. Preregistered but **deviated from** (deviation made before Stage B ran, after Stage A timing was known)
- Stage B was cut from **2 device noise models to 1** (`dev_lagos`) to fit the compute budget. Seeds (5) and SPSA iterations (40) were preserved. Recorded in `configs/stage_b.yaml`.
- **Consequence:** RQ3 has no noise-model replication at all. A `dev_algiers` in-loop run could plausibly reverse the Stage B ordering, exactly as the noise model reverses the Stage A winner (§5). **RQ3 should be treated as single-condition.**

### 2c. **Exploratory / post-hoc — added after inspecting data, must not be treated as confirmatory**
- **The unphysical-estimate metric** (Stage A §5.6 of HANDOFF, and the in-loop `unphysical_pct`). This was not preregistered. It was added after a smoke-test estimate exceeded the exact maximum cut. The counts are real and computed from committed raw data, but the metric was **chosen after seeing that it would be interesting**.
- **The `observed_descent` / in-loop diagnostics table.** Added after inspecting 19 of 40 Stage B runs.
- **The proposed mechanism** (order-preservation; see §6).
- **The noise-model-dependence finding** (§5). Preregistered as exploratory EQ2, so correctly labelled, but it is now doing load-bearing work in the recommendation and should be confirmed in a dedicated experiment before publication.

> **Reviewer note:** the most quotable findings of this pilot (unphysical estimates, the in-loop
> mechanism) are in category **2c**. They are hypotheses generated by this pilot, not results
> confirmed by it.

## 3. Absolute error vs. ratios — **correction to earlier framing**

The earlier HANDOFF text reported Stage B as "ZNE 5× worse, REM 11× worse, ZNE+REM 167× worse."
Those ratios are arithmetically correct but **computed against a near-zero baseline** (0.0046 on a
cut scale whose maximum is 7). Stated in absolute terms, most of the effect disappears:

### Stage B, QAOA p=1 (baseline absolute error 0.0046; problem scale C_max = 7)

| Method | absolute error | ratio | absolute difference | **as % of problem scale** | practically meaningful? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| none | 0.0046 | 1.0× | — | — | baseline |
| ZNE | 0.0236 | 5.2× | +0.019 | **+0.27%** | **no** |
| REM | 0.0527 | 11.6× | +0.048 | **+0.69%** | **no** |
| ZNE+REM | 0.7669 | 168× | +0.762 | **+10.9%** | **yes** |

**Corrected claim:** on the converged QAOA task, only **ZNE+REM** degrades the optimisation result
by a practically meaningful amount. ZNE and REM are *statistically* distinguishable from the
baseline (disjoint CIs) but degrade the final objective by **under 1% of the problem scale**. The
"11× worse" phrasing was misleading and has been removed from `HANDOFF.md`.

### Stage B, TFIM (baseline 3.089; scale |E₀| = 4.759) — all CIs overlap, inconclusive

| Method | absolute error | ratio | absolute difference | as % of scale |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| REM | 2.884 | 0.93× | −0.205 | −4.3% |
| none | 3.089 | 1.0× | — | — |
| ZNE | 3.134 | 1.01× | +0.045 | +0.9% |
| ZNE+REM | 4.277 | 1.38× | +1.188 | +25.0% |

### Stage A: the same caution applies to the ideal-noise "mitigation hurts" result

The ideal-noise ratios (REM 1.13×, ZNE 1.71×, ZNE+REM 2.09×) are computed against baselines of
**0.006–0.046 absolute**. The direction is theoretically required and the pipeline check is valid,
but the magnitudes are negligible in absolute terms. Three Stage A cells show ratios > 2× with
absolute differences under 0.02:

| task | noise | budget | method | base | mitigated | ratio | abs diff |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| qaoa_p1 | ideal | 12000 | zne | 0.0058 | 0.0177 | 3.07× | +0.0119 |
| qaoa_p1 | ideal | 12000 | zne_rem | 0.0058 | 0.0186 | 3.22× | +0.0128 |
| tfim | ideal | 12000 | zne_rem | 0.0123 | 0.0269 | 2.19× | +0.0147 |

**Rule adopted for any future write-up:** every ratio must be accompanied by the absolute difference
and the problem scale. Ratios alone are not reportable.

| none | 9300.0 | 1.5 | 5,580,000 | 600 |

## 5. Noise-model dependence (exploratory EQ2) — the result that most constrains the others

At the largest budget the **best method depends on which noise model is used**:

| noise family | best method |
|---|---|
| `dev_lagos`, `dev_algiers` (device calibration snapshots) | REM (one cell: ZNE+REM) |
| `param_lam{0.5, 1, 2}` (hand-built parametric) | ZNE+REM, consistently |

A study using only the parametric model would conclude ZNE+REM is best; one using only device
snapshots would conclude REM is best. **This is direct evidence that single-noise-model mitigation
studies are unreliable — including, by the same logic, our own single-noise-model Stage B (§2b).**

## 6. Known defects a reviewer should press on

### 6.1 **ZNE noise scaling is misspecified** (material — may invalidate the ZNE arm)

Richardson extrapolation is fitted against nominal scale factors (1, 2, 3). The **achieved
two-qubit-gate amplification**, recorded per-cell in the raw data (`cx_per_scale`), is not:

| task | basis | CX @ nominal 1 | @ nominal 2 | @ nominal 3 | achieved ratio @ nominal 2 | achieved @ 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| qaoa_p1 | z | 18 | 30 | 54 | **1.80** | 3.00 |
| tfim | x | 3 | 5 | 9 | **1.82** | 3.00 |
| tfim | z | 3 | 7 | 9 | **2.38** | 3.00 |

`fold_global` scales *total gate count* by the nominal factor, but CX gates — which dominate the
error budget — are amplified by a different, circuit-dependent factor. The endpoint (×3) is exact;
**the midpoint is off by −10% to +19%**, and the midpoint is exactly what fixes the curvature in a
three-point Richardson fit.

Worse, **the two measurement bases of the same TFIM observable scale inconsistently** (1.82 vs 2.38),
so the two halves of the energy are extrapolated under different effective noise.

**Implication:** the ZNE arm's poor showing may be partly an artifact of misspecified scaling rather
than an intrinsic property of ZNE. **This must be resolved before any claim about ZNE is published.**
Fix: fit against measured CX-count ratios, or use a folding method that scales two-qubit gates
proportionally, and re-run the ZNE and ZNE+REM arms.

### 6.2 Multiple comparisons, uncorrected
60 comparison groups per method, no family-wise correction. At α = 0.05, ≈3 false "helps" are
expected by chance. REM's 46 is far above that; individual cell verdicts are **not** individually
reliable. The phrase "never harms" (0/60) is under-powered at 10 seeds and should not appear in a
manuscript without ≥30 seeds and correction.

### 6.3 TFIM in-loop did not converge
SPSA, 40 iterations, 16 parameters, random start. All arms end 2.9–4.3 from a reference of −4.734;
every CI overlaps the baseline. **No TFIM in-loop conclusion is supported.** Half of Stage B is
therefore uninformative.

### 6.4 One pathological qubit carries weight
`dev_lagos` qubit 2 has a **46.5% readout error**, making the confusion-matrix inversion
ill-conditioned (~14× variance amplification). It plausibly drives REM's small-budget variance on
that device. A re-run excluding qubit 2 is needed to show the REM result is not an artifact of it.

### 6.5 Unvaried confounders
One ansatz per task; SPSA only; global folding only; Richardson only in confirmatory arms;
`cx`-native IBM superconducting devices only; 4–6 qubits.

## 7. Direct evidence vs. proposed explanation

**Clearly separated, because the pilot's most repeatable-sounding sentence is an untested hypothesis.**

### Direct evidence (measured, in committed raw data, reproducible by re-running `analyze.py`)
- Stage A verdict counts and error magnitudes (§1, §3).
- On the ideal simulator, all mitigation arms have larger absolute error than no mitigation.
- Stage B final-parameter errors on QAOA (§3), CIs disjoint from baseline for all three mitigated arms.
- 161/2400 Stage A estimates and 10.25%/3.25% of Stage B in-loop evaluations were physically impossible.
- Shot and execution counts (§4). Achieved CX amplification per scale factor (§6.1).

### Proposed explanation — **hypothesis, not a finding**
> *An optimiser needs an objective whose ordering of parameter points is preserved, not one that is
> unbiased. Noise-induced bias in these tasks is close to a monotone transformation of the true
> objective, so SPSA descends correctly without mitigation; mitigation trades that harmless bias for
> variance in every gradient estimate, which a stochastic optimiser cannot absorb.*

**What supports it:** the co-occurrence of (a) mitigated arms losing in-loop while winning at fixed
parameters, and (b) ZNE+REM having both the highest unphysical rate and the worst final parameters.

**What does NOT support it — the tests that were never run:**
- Monotonicity of the noisy objective in the true objective was **never measured**. This is the load-bearing assumption and it is untested.
- No experiment varied variance while holding bias fixed (or vice versa), so the causal claim is unidentified.
- Variance of the *gradient estimate* was never recorded — only variance of the objective estimate across seeds.
- The alternative explanation — that unphysical over-correction alone drives it — is not separated from the variance explanation. ZNE+REM is confounded: it is simultaneously the highest-variance arm *and* the most unphysical one.
- A simpler alternative — SPSA hyperparameters (`a`, `c`) were tuned for none of the arms and may simply suit the low-variance unmitigated objective — was not ruled out.

**Recommended framing until tested:** "mitigation improved estimation and did not improve
optimisation in this single noise condition; the mechanism is unresolved."

## 8. What this pilot does and does not license

**Supported by the frozen data:**
- Mitigation methods differ substantially once total shot cost is equalised, and the ordering is not the conventional one.
- The ordering depends on the noise model (§5).
- Unconstrained Richardson extrapolation produces physically impossible values at a non-trivial rate.
- The estimation-stage ranking did not transfer to the single in-loop condition tested.

**Not supported — do not write these:**
- "ZNE is harmful." §6.1 may explain the ZNE arm's behaviour as an implementation artifact.
- "REM never harms." Under-powered; uncorrected multiple comparisons.
- "Mitigation hurts optimisation." One noise model, one converged task, and the practically meaningful effect is confined to ZNE+REM (§3).
- Any statement about hardware, larger systems, or quantum advantage.
- Any causal mechanism claim (§7).

**Literature-gap status:** unchanged from `docs/novelty-gap.md` — rated **moderate**, bounded by an
incomplete search (arXiv API was rate-limited; no systematic sweep; no forward-citation analysis of
Bultrini 2023 / Russo 2023). **The gap claim has not been re-verified since 2026-09-15** and should
be re-checked as part of this review.
